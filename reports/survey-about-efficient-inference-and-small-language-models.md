# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Survey of the Memory Wall, Decoding Algorithms, Model Recipes, and Serving Systems

## TL;DR

- **Decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound, not compute-bound.** At batch size 1 a BF16 model performs roughly 1 FLOP per weight byte moved, against an H100 ridge point of ~295 FLOP/B, so single-stream decode runs at well under 1% of peak compute; latency is essentially model bytes divided by HBM bandwidth (e.g. ~42 ms/token for a 70B FP16 model) [1][2]. Even at group size 8 with GQA, attention intensity (~8 FLOP/B) remains more than an order of magnitude below the ridge, and 2-bit KV quantization only lifts the KV floor to ~64 FLOP/B [1].
- **KV-cache traffic, not weight traffic, becomes the binding constraint at long context.** The crossover batch size at which KV I/O overtakes weight I/O is roughly 108 for FP16 weights + FP16 KV on Llama-3-70B, but INT4 weight quantization moves it ~27x to the left, and because the crossover is inversely proportional to sequence length, long-context inference stays KV-dominated even at batch size 1 [1][2].
- **Compression and speculation deliver multiplicative, largely output-preserving speedups.** PagedAttention/vLLM improves serving throughput 2–4x at equal latency [3] and up to 24x over HuggingFace Transformers [4]; MLA in DeepSeek-V2 cuts KV cache by 93.3% and raises max generation throughput 5.76x [5]; EAGLE-3 reaches up to 6.5x over vanilla autoregressive decoding [6]; AWQ enables W4A16 with >3x speedup [7]; Gemma 2's 2B/9B are trained by distillation rather than next-token prediction [8].
- **Small models have been decoupled from Chinchilla-optimal scaling by data quality and over-training.** phi-1 (1.3B) reaches 50.6% HumanEval with ~7B curated tokens [9]; SmolLM2-1.7B is overtrained on ~11T tokens (~$250K of compute) and Phi-3-mini (3.8B) reaches 69% MMLU and 8.38 MT-Bench while running on a phone [10][11]. MiniCPM's fitted scaling law puts the compute-optimal data-to-model ratio near 192:1 versus Chinchilla's ~20:1 [12].
- **The economically decisive shift is that inference now dominates AI compute** (roughly two-thirds of all AI compute, 80–90% of lifetime cost in production) [13], while the capabilities of sub-3B models still break down on multi-step reasoning, knowledge-intensive QA and long context, and low-bit quantization interacts badly with heavily-trained small models [14][15][16].

## Background

### The memory wall of autoregressive inference

Decoder-only generation proceeds in two phases with opposite hardware profiles. Prefill consumes the whole prompt and builds the KV cache with large, compute-bound matmuls (arithmetic intensity roughly scales with context length, ~340 FLOP/byte at context 1024), whereas decode emits one token at a time with arithmetic intensity of about 1 FLOP/byte and is memory-bound at every realistic operating point [1][2]. A roofline analysis of Llama-2-7B confirms that nearly all computations are compute-bound during prefill but uniformly memory-bound during decode, and because prefill runs once while decode repeats per output token, decode dominates the optimization budget [2].

The quantitative root cause is simple: a forward pass over `P` parameters does ~`2P` FLOPs while streaming ~`P·w_p` weight bytes, giving intensity `2/w_p` — approximately 1 FLOP/B for BF16 [1]. The H100 SXM ridge point is ~295 FLOP/B (989.5 TFLOP/s dense BF16 over 3.35 TB/s HBM3), so single-stream decode sits at ~1/295 of the ridge; the floor for 2-bit KV cache reads is ~64 FLOP/B, still ~4.6x below the ridge, which leads to the blunt conclusion that no realistic storage precision makes decode compute-bound [1]. Concretely, a 70B FP16 model needs ~140 GB of weights and, with a 128K-token sequence, ~42 GB of KV cache — more than the 80 GB of a single accelerator, so the deployment is multi-GPU by arithmetic necessity [1].

Beyond a hardware- and context-dependent crossover batch size `B*`, KV-cache I/O overtakes weight I/O: `B* ≈ 108` for FP16 weights with FP16 KV, ~27x smaller under INT4 weight quantization, and back to ~106 when weights *and* KV are both INT4; since `B*` is inversely proportional to sequence length, contexts of 128K are always KV-dominated [1]. The aggregate effect is that the KV cache has become the "new memory wall," and batching — the classical throughput lever — loses its power for long context because KV bytes scale with batch size exactly as attention FLOPs do [1]. Two-phase service metrics formalize the split: TTFT is governed by prefill and TPOT by decode; for Llama-3-70B FP16 at B=32, s=4096 the model must move ~183 GB (141 GB weights + 42 GB KV) per step, giving TPOT ≈ 68 ms at 80% of peak bandwidth [1]. Systems such as DistServe respond by physically disaggregating prefill and decode onto different GPUs to optimize goodput under latency SLOs [17].

### Scaling laws and the case for small, heavily-trained models

Chinchilla trained over 400 LMs from 70M to 16B parameters on 5B–500B tokens and concluded that contemporary LLMs were significantly undertrained: for compute-optimal training, parameter count and training tokens should scale equally, with both exponents ≈0.50 ("for every doubling of model size the number of training tokens should also be doubled") [18]. The empirical payoff was that a 70B model trained on 1.4T tokens matched Gopher's (280B) compute budget while uniformly outperforming Gopher, GPT-3 (175B), Jurassic-1 (178B) and Megatron-Turing NLG (530B), reaching 67.5% average on MMLU — more than 7 points above Gopher [18]. Chinchilla was explicitly motivated by deployment efficiency: a smaller, better-trained model lowers inference cost and can be run on smaller hardware, and because an LLM's energy cost is amortized through inference rather than training, the efficiency argument is economic as much as statistical [18].

The counter-current is data-centric scaling. phi-1, a 1.3B Transformer trained for four days on 8 A100s with only ~7B tokens of "textbook quality" web data plus 1B tokens of GPT-3.5-generated textbooks and exercises, reaches 50.6% pass@1 on HumanEval and 55.5% on MBPP, while a 350M phi-1-small still reaches 45% HumanEval [9]. phi-1.5 extended the recipe to common-sense reasoning with a 30B-token predominantly synthetic dataset, matching models 10x larger trained on >10x the data and exhibiting step-by-step reasoning and rudimentary in-context learning [19]. TinyLlama trained a 1.1B model on ~3T tokens (~3 epochs over a ~950B-token SlimPajama+StarCoder 7:3 mix) on 16 A100-40G GPUs, explicitly testing what happens when a 1B model is trained far past the scaling-law prescription, and beat OPT-1.3B and Pythia-1.4B [20]. MiniCPM's fitted scaling law makes the trade-off quantitative: the compute-optimal data-to-model ratio is ~192:1, roughly 10x Chinchilla's ~20:1 [12].

These results motivate the small-language-model (SLM) literature proper, surveyed with a two-axis taxonomy of (i) architecture, training and compression and (ii) the constrained resource being optimized — inference compute, training time, speed, memory, storage or latency [21]. Practically, "small" now means 135M–3B parameters [22], motivated by latency, cost, privacy and edge deployment: Llama 3.2's 1B/3B text models were designed to run on Qualcomm/MediaTek hardware and optimized for Arm processors, with Meta emphasizing that data never leaves the device [23]; Apple Intelligence runs a ~3B on-device model; Gemini Nano-1 (1.8B) and Nano-2 (3.25B) are distilled, 4-bit quantized variants of the Gemini 1.0 family [24][25][26]. The efficiency payoff is measurable: quantized Llama 3.2 1B/3B variants achieve 2–4x speedup with ~56% average model-size reduction and ~41% memory reduction vs BF16, and on a OnePlus 12 delivered 2.5x better decode latency and 4.2x better prefill latency on average up to 8K context [27].

## Decoding and KV-cache efficiency: what actually buys speed

### KV-cache reduction: latent compression, quantization, eviction, paging

The KV cache is the most attacked target because it gates both memory and batch size. At the architecture level, Multi-head Latent Attention in DeepSeek-V2 compresses per-token KV into a low-rank latent vector of size `(d_c + d_h^R)·l ≈ 4.5·d_h·l` — equivalent to GQA with only 2.25 groups but with stronger-than-MHA quality — reducing KV cache by 93.3% versus DeepSeek 67B and raising maximum generation throughput to 5.76x (over 50K tokens/s on 8xH800) [5]. Where the architecture is frozen, quantization and eviction apply. KIVI is a tuning-free 2-bit KV scheme that quantizes keys per-channel and values per-token (asymmetric), yielding 2.6x peak-memory reduction for Llama-2-7B at ~2% GSM8K accuracy loss and up to 4x larger batch size with 2.35–3.47x throughput [28]. A radical alternative is eviction: StreamingLLM keeps the KV of only the 4 initial "attention sink" tokens plus a rolling window, letting finite-window models attend over 4M tokens with no fine-tuning and delivering up to 22.2x speedup over a sliding-window baseline with recomputation [29]. H2O formalizes eviction as dynamic submodular maximization, retaining roughly 20% heavy hitters plus recent tokens for up to 29x throughput over DeepSpeed Zero-Inference and 3x over FlexGen [30]. SnapKV instead selects clustered important KV positions per head from an observation window at the end of the prompt, obtaining 3.6x generation speedup and 8.2x memory efficiency at 16K inputs with negligible accuracy loss, and scaling to as much as 380K context on a single A100-80GB [31]. At the systems layer, PagedAttention manages the KV cache in non-contiguous fixed-size blocks with near-zero waste, improving serving throughput 2–4x at equal latency over Orca and FasterTransformer [3], with under 4% memory waste and up to 55% sampling-memory savings in the reported vLLM deployment figures [4].

### Speculative decoding and cheaper attention kernels

Speculative decoding trades extra compute (which is idle anyway) for fewer sequential memory-bound steps. Medusa attaches multiple decoding heads to the backbone and decodes candidates with tree attention: Medusa-1 gives >2.2x speedup with no quality loss and Medusa-2, co-trained with the backbone, reaches 2.3–2.8x [32]. EAGLE-3 drops feature prediction in favor of direct token prediction with multi-layer feature fusion ("training-time test"), reaching up to 6.5x over vanilla autoregressive decoding — 1.4x over EAGLE-2 at batch size 1 — with average acceptance lengths up to 7.5 on HumanEval and 1.38x throughput inside SGLang at batch size 64 [6]. Self-speculation removes the draft model entirely: LayerSkip uses layer dropout plus an early-exit loss so the early layers draft and the remaining layers verify while sharing a single KV cache, reaching up to 2.16x on CNN/DM summarization, 1.82x on coding and 2.0x on TOPv2 [33]. Recent work fuses compression with speculation: QuantSpec uses a hierarchical 4-bit quantized KV cache and weights for self-speculative decoding on edge devices [34], and LongSpec maintains a constant-sized KV cache with novel position indices for lossless long-context speculative decoding [35].

Attention kernels and sequence-mixing alternatives bound the compute side. FlashAttention-2 improves work partitioning to reach ~2x over FlashAttention (1.7–3.0x) and 3–10x over standard attention, hitting 230 TFLOP/s (73% of A100 peak) with 225 TFLOP/s end-to-end GPT training (72% MFU) and linear rather than quadratic memory [36]. Mamba's selective state-space formulation gives linear-time scaling, 5x higher inference throughput than Transformers, and constant-time per-step decoding with no KV cache to store, with Mamba-3B matching Transformers 2x its size [37]. Hybrid designs hedge: Jamba interleaves Transformer and Mamba layers with MoE, obtaining an 8x smaller KV cache than a vanilla Transformer and 3x higher throughput than Mixtral-8x7B at 128K context with support to 256K [38].

### Weight quantization and distillation into small models

AWQ shows that protecting only ~1% of salient weight channels, identified from activation distributions, enables 4-bit weight-only (W4A16) quantization with >3x desktop/mobile GPU speedup [7]. SmoothQuant migrates activation outliers into weights via an equivalent per-channel scaling to enable W8A8, giving up to 1.56x speedup and 2x memory reduction and fitting a 530B model inside a single 8-GPU node [39]. Distillation is the other route to small capability: Gemma 2 trains its 2B and 9B models by minimizing teacher-student negative log-likelihood for >50x the compute-optimal token count, improving a 2B model's 3-benchmark average from 60.3 to 67.7 at matched tokens and beating from-scratch training as student size grows [8]. MiniLLM argues that forward KL is the wrong objective for generative LLM distillation because the student over-estimates the teacher's low-probability regions, and shows reverse KL yields more precise, better-calibrated students from 120M to 13B [40]. Cheaper still is reuse rather than retraining: Sheared LLaMA prunes LLaMA2-7B to 1.3B/2.7B with targeted structured pruning plus 50B continued-pretraining tokens, using only 3% of the compute of training from scratch while beating Pythia/INCITE/OpenLLaMA/TinyLlama at equivalent sizes [41], and model soups average independently fine-tuned weights to gain accuracy and robustness with no extra inference cost — a ViT-G soup reached 90.94% ImageNet top-1 with 25% fewer inference FLOPs [42].

## Small language models: recipes, capabilities, and the parameter-capability frontier

### Data-centric over-training: Phi, Qwen2.5, Llama-3.2, SmolLM2

The Phi line is the clearest demonstration that token *quality* can substitute for scale. phi-1 (1.3B, ~7B tokens) reaches 50.6% HumanEval and 55.5% MBPP [9]; phi-1.5 (1.3B, 24 layers, context 2048) reaches common-sense reasoning comparable to models 10x larger with a 30B-token mostly-synthetic dataset [19]; Phi-2 (2.7B, 1.4T tokens, 2048 context) matches or beats models up to 25x larger, scoring 59.2 BBH and 53.7 on coding versus Llama-2-70B's 38.3 coding [43]; Phi-3-mini (3.8B, 3.3T tokens, 8K context with GQA and block-sparse attention alternating with dense attention to cut KV-cache) reaches 69% MMLU, 8.38 MT-Bench and 82.5 GSM8K 8-shot CoT while deployable on a phone [11]. The Qwen2.5 family spans 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B/14B/32B/72B with 32K context and tied embeddings in the small sizes; Qwen2.5-0.5B scores 47.5 MMLU and 30.5 HumanEval, 1.5B scores 60.9 MMLU / 68.5 GSM8K / 37.2 HumanEval, and 3B reaches 65.6 MMLU, 79.1 GSM8K and 42.1 HumanEval — notably, Qwen2.5-0.5B outperforms Gemma2-2.6B on math and coding despite being ~5x smaller [44][45]. Llama-3.2's 1B (1.23B) and 3B (3.21B) text models train on up to 9T tokens with 128K context and GQA/shared embeddings, scoring 32.2 and 58 MMLU respectively [46][23].

SmolLM2 pushes over-training further with a multi-stage data recipe: SmolLM2-1.7B is overtrained on ~11T tokens (~2 epochs) for roughly 1e23 FLOPs / ~$250K on 256 H100s, with 360M on 4T tokens and 135M on 2T tokens using GQA and a single-stage high-quality mixture [10]. SmolLM2-1.7B base beats Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama-3.2-1B on HellaSwag (68.7 vs 66.4/61.2), MMLU-Pro (19.4 vs 13.7) and TriviaQA (36.7 vs 20.9), and SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct reaches IFEval 56.7 versus Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct's 47.4 with 100% needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at 8K — but it lags badly on GSM8K (31.1 vs 61.7) and HumanEval (22.6 vs 37.2), isolating exactly where the recipe does not transfer [10].

### Architecture over data at the sub-billion scale, and distillation on-device

Below one billion parameters the balance shifts toward architecture. MobileLLM shows that for sub-billion models architecture matters more than data or parameter quantity, using deep-and-thin ("lanky") designs with SwiGLU FFN, embedding sharing and GQA to gain +2.7%/+4.3% over prior 125M/350M state of the art, with block-wise layer sharing (MobileLLM-LS) adding a further +0.7%/+0.8% at no size cost; MobileLLM-LS-350M achieves a 48.2% win rate against GPT-3 (text-davinci-001) on chat and approaches LLaMA-v2-7B correctness on API-calling tasks [47]. OpenELM applies layer-wise (non-uniform) scaling so that ~1B parameters improve accuracy 2.36% over OLMo-1.2B while using 2x fewer pre-training tokens [48]. StableLM-2-1.6B trains ~2T multilingual tokens over two epochs at 4096 context and tops the open <2B class at publication [49]. On-device, Apple's ~3B model shares KV cache across blocks 1 and 2 to cut KV memory and TTFT by ~37.5% and uses 2-bit quantization-aware training with 4-bit embeddings and 8-bit KV, but is explicitly specialized for summarization, entity extraction and short dialog rather than world knowledge [24]; Gemini Nano-2 (3.25B, distilled and 4-bit quantized) scores MMLU 55.8, BBH 42.4, MBPP 27.2 and MATH 22.8 — comparable to roughly 0.45–0.90 of Gemini Pro's performance at a fraction of the size, with LoRA adapters for feature-specific Android APIs [25][26].

### Where small models still fail, and the safety side of efficiency

The Gemma team notes that the latest small models need up to 15T tokens to move the state of the art by under 1–2% and remain under-trained [8]; more pointedly, capability gaps concentrate in multi-step math/coding and knowledge-intensive QA, where 1.7B-class models trail 1.5B-class Qwen models by 30 points on GSM8K [10] and Phi-3 shows a regression on HumanEval (55.5) with diminishing returns from 7B to 14B [11]. On a 16-topic structured multiple-choice benchmark of 1,085 examples, 135M–3B models separate sharply (Qwen Coder 3B at 75.67% versus SmolLM2-135M at 30.05%), but parameter-efficient fine-tuning with 4-bit NF4 plus LoRA/DoRA recovers large gains (+26.85 points for Qwen Coder 3B, +25.92 for SmolLM2-1.7B), indicating that specialized adaptation can make sub-3B models credible local experts [21][22]. Efficiency also imports risk: dedicated studies find small models disproportionately vulnerable to jailbreak and membership-inference attacks [50].

## Serving systems, benchmarks, and deployment economics

### Engines and accelerators: the reported throughput frontier

Engine-level results cluster tightly around KV-memory management and batching. vLLM's PagedAttention reports 2–4x throughput over Orca and FasterTransformer at equal latency [3] and up to 24x over HuggingFace Transformers with up to 3.5x over TGI in the released benchmarks [4]. SGLang's RadixAttention reuses KV prefixes through a radix tree with LRU eviction, reporting up to 5x over Guidance and vLLM in the blog benchmark [51] and up to 6.4x higher throughput / 3.7x lower latency with 50–99% cache hit rates and cache-aware scheduling reaching ~96% of optimal in the paper [52]. On the accelerator side, TensorRT-LLM on H100 delivers 4.6x faster Llama-2-70B inference than A100 (5.3x lower TCO, 5.6x energy savings on GPT-J-6B) [53], 10,907 output tok/s for GPT-J-6B at batch 64 in FP8 versus 3,679 tok/s on A100 FP16 with first-token latency of 102 ms versus 481 ms [54], and ~21,000 tokens/s offline on Mixtral 8x7B with FP8 giving ~50% more throughput than FP16 within a 0.5 s response limit [55]. Extending downward, FlexGen runs OPT-175B on a single 16 GB GPU at 1 token/s with effective batch size 144 and, with 4-bit compression, achieves 69x (up to 112x) the throughput of DeepSpeed ZeRO-Inference [56]; PowerInfer exploits neuron activation locality in a GPU-CPU hybrid to beat llama.cpp by up to 11.69x on one RTX 4090 (13.20 tok/s average, 29.08 tok/s peak) and reach 82% of an A100's generation rate on OPT-30B [57]; MLC-LLM compiles 4-bit Llama2-70B to 34.5 tok/s on 2x RTX 4090 and unifies continuous batching, speculative decoding, paged KV, prefix caching and cascade inference across iOS/Android/WebGPU [58].

### Benchmarks: what is measured, and the measurement gap

Standardized harnesses now separate the datacenter, client and edge regimes. MLPerf Client evaluates LLMs, image models and agentic tasks on PCs with TTFT and tokens/s, int4-quantized weights, and accuracy gated by MMLU and IFEval [59]; Datacenter and Edge suites define the throughput/latency scenarios in which the TensorRT-LLM and vLLM numbers above are reported [60]. On the quality side, Chatbot Arena's Elo/Bradley-Terry ratings were an early signal that size was not destiny: Vicuna-7B reached Elo 1007 versus Vicuna-13B at 1054 (May 2023), and by December 2023 7B models (OpenChat-3.5 at 1077, Starling-LM-7B-alpha at 1083) were performing favorably against doubled-parameter models, with LMSYS speculating that pretraining/fine-tuning data quality mattered more than scale for these tasks [61][62]. Long-context claims are where benchmark reality diverges most sharply: RULER showed that despite near-perfect needle-in-a-haystack scores, only about half of models claiming ≥32K context maintain satisfactory performance at 32K, with GPT-4's effective length at 64K and Qwen2-72B at 32K [16]. This matters for small models because effective context degrades with size, and because KV traffic — not compute — is the limiter.

### Edge and on-device: bandwidth, not FLOPS

The edge regime is a purer instance of the same memory wall. Apple's M5 at 153 GB/s delivers only 19–27% higher generation speed than the M4 at 120 GB/s despite up to 4x faster TTFT from Neural Accelerators, because generation is bandwidth-bound while TTFT is compute-bound [63]. A systematic Apple Silicon characterization found that inference latency is not proportional to precision (a 2-bit model can be slower than a 1-bit one) due to dequantization overhead, ALU cost and the absence of dedicated tensor cores, while unified memory makes Apple Silicon increasingly cost-effective for ≥70B models [64]. At 128K context, a Q2 model on an M3 Ultra is only 1.7x faster than F16 (down from 4.6x at 1K) because the always-FP16 KV cache dominates bandwidth [64]. Qualcomm states explicitly that memory bandwidth rather than processing is the bottleneck for token generation, noting Snapdragon 8 Gen 3's native INT4 support and 77 GB/s LPDDR5x with Llama 2-7B at up to 20 tokens/s, and X Elite's 135 GB/s / 45 TOPS NPU [65]. Google's LiteRT QNN path on Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 (int8 weights, int16 activations) runs FastVLM-0.5B at 0.12 s TTFT on 1024x1024 images with >11,000 tokens/s prefill and >100 tokens/s decode, up to 100x CPU and 10x GPU speedups across 72 models [66].

### Cost economics

Inference has crossed from a minority to the majority of AI compute: it is now roughly two-thirds of all AI compute (up from ~one-third in 2023 and ~half in 2025), can be 80–90% of a production system's lifetime cost, and unit prices have collapsed — the Stanford AI Index trajectory cited runs from ~$20 to ~$0.07 per million tokens [13]. DeepSeek-V3 demonstrates the sparse alternative: a 671B-total / 37B-active MoE with MLA trained in 2.788M H800 GPU-hours (~$5.576M at $2/GPU-hour), serving at $0.2574/M input and $1.029/M output tokens [67][68]. The interaction between sparse architectures, aggressive quantization and small dense models is now the central design question in serving economics.

## Trends and open problems

**1. The bottleneck has moved from weights to the KV cache, and serving stacks have not fully caught up.** Weight-vs-KV crossover `B*` is ~27x smaller under INT4 weights [1], and long context makes KV dominant even at batch 1 [1][2]. Yet KV quantization must be applied asymmetrically (per-channel keys, per-token values) to work at 2 bits [28], eviction methods trade accuracy for memory in ways that are task-dependent [29][30][31], and at 128K context an always-FP16 KV cache erases most of the benefit of weight quantization on consumer hardware [64]. Unified weight-and-KV low-bit schemes with speculation (e.g. QuantSpec, LongSpec) are the emerging answer [34][35].

**2. Low-bit quantization systematically favors *under*-trained models, which is in tension with the over-training recipe that makes small models good.** Across 1,500+ quantized checkpoints, larger and more undertrained models show less quantization-induced degradation, while smaller well-trained models degrade more, and scaling projections warn that 2–3 bit quantization may perform poorly for future 100T-token models [15]. Local-error PTQ (GPTQ) and global-loss quantization-aware fine-tuning are fundamentally misaligned, most severely at very low precision, and QAFT dominates GPTQ under matched data budgets, with even one epoch of QAFT beating GPTQ [69]. For reasoning models the gap is dramatic: on Qwen3-0.6B, reasoning-aware QAT beats GPTQ by 44.53 points on MATH-500, and at 2 bits R1-Qwen-1.5B improves MATH-500 from 3.67% to 55.00% [69]. Meanwhile, quantized 4-bit LLMs generally outperform smaller dense models yet degrade on hallucination detection and instruction-following relative to half precision, and weight-only quantization (AWQ over GPTQ) preserved accuracy better than activation quantization in Llama-3.1-405B [70]. The open question is whether the industry's two dominant recipes — over-train small and compress — are mutually compatible at 2–3 bits.

**3. Small models have a hard reasoning ceiling that distillation alone does not raise.** The "Small Model Learnability Gap" shows models ≤3B parameters do not consistently benefit from long chain-of-thought data or distillation from large teachers, performing better with short, simple chains; mix-distillation recovered >8-point gains for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on MATH and AMC [14]. And Gemma 2's own report notes that small models need up to 15T tokens to yield <1–2% headline improvements while remaining under-trained [8] — i.e., the data-centric lever is showing diminishing returns unless the objective changes. Reverse-KL distillation [40] and distillation-based training of whole families [8] are the strongest current answers, but the field has no accepted account of which capabilities can be distilled into a 1–3B student.

**4. Evaluation is the weakest link, especially for long context and for efficiency claims.** Effective context is far below claimed context for roughly half of ≥32K models [16], efficiency numbers are usually reported under engine-specific, non-comparable conditions (batch sizes, sequence lengths, hardware, precision), and community-facing harnesses such as MLPerf Client were only recently defined with accuracy gates [59][60]. The Arena trajectory suggests data quality can outweigh parameter count for chat-style tasks [61][62], but chat Elo and reasoning benchmarks disagree in ways that no current small-model leaderboard resolves.

**5. Efficiency is not cost-free: safety, energy and fairness.** Small models are disproportionately susceptible to jailbreaks and membership-inference attacks [50]; quantization changes failure modes rather than merely degrading them [70]; and the energy story is double-edged, since inference is now the dominant share of AI compute and of data-center power [13]. Whether sub-3B, 4-bit, on-device models can be made robust enough for privacy-sensitive deployment — the very reason they are attractive [23][26] — remains unresolved.
