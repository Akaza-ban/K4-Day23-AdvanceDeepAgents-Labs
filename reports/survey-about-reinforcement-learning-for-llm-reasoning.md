# Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning: Algorithms, Verifiers, Test-Time Compute, and the Open Debate on What RLVR Actually Learns

## TL;DR

- Reinforcement learning (RL) for LLM reasoning re-frames autoregressive generation as a token-level MDP whose reward is delivered once, at the end of a long chain of thought; the entire algorithmic history of the field is a sequence of attempts to build low-variance, unbiased estimators of that single sparse signal [1][2][3][4].
- The engineering trajectory is clear: PPO with a learned critic [1] gave way to critic-free group/leave-one-out baselines (GRPO, RLOO) that roughly halve actor-side memory and are simpler to tune [2][5], and then to bias-corrected and importance-sampling-corrected variants (DAPO, Dr. GRPO, GSPO, TIS/TOPR) that explicitly repair known defects in the GRPO objective [6][7][8][9][10].
- Reward and verification infrastructure, not the optimizer, is the binding constraint: process reward models (PRMs) beat outcome-only supervision on MATH (78.2% solved) [11][12], yet rule-based verifiers produce false negatives that worsen as the policy improves while model-based verifiers are hackable through false positives [13][14][15].
- Test-time compute and train-time RL are two dials on the same system: o1 moves from 74% to 83% to 93% on 2024 AIME as samples grow from 1 to 64 to 1000 with re-ranking [16], s1's budget forcing lifts AIME24 from 50% to 57% [17], and OpenAI reports that large-scale RL exhibits the same "more compute = better performance" trend as pretraining [18].
- The most consequential open debate is whether RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) discovers new reasoning or merely sharpens what the base model already contains: pass@k studies report that base models overtake RL-trained policies at large k and that shrinkage of empirical support generally outweighs expansion, with a reported ratio of roughly 3.6:1 [19][20], while ProRL-style prolonged RL argues the boundary does expand with sufficient compute and task diversity [21], with entropy collapse as the focal mechanism on both sides [22][23][24].

## Background

**Why RL at all.** Supervised fine-tuning minimizes token-level cross-entropy, which is only an indirect proxy for sequence-level qualities such as correctness, and it suffers exposure bias because training conditions on gold prefixes while inference conditions on self-generated ones [3]. RL instead samples multiple candidate reasoning trajectories and reinforces those with higher outcome-level, process-level, or verifier-derived rewards, directly optimizing the quantity we care about [3][4].

**The token-level MDP and the credit-assignment problem.** An LLM acts as a policy that emits a chain of thought as a sequence of actions; the state is the context window, the action space is the discrete vocabulary, and the reward is usually a single scalar at the end of the trajectory [3][4]. The context window is high-dimensional and the vocabulary is vast and discrete, so reparameterization-trick estimators are inapplicable and the field relies on policy-gradient methods with KL-constrained generation [3]. Because one terminal reward must be attributed to thousands of tokens, credit assignment is the structural difficulty that motivates group-relative baselines and sequence-level ratios rather than per-token value estimation [2][5][8].

**The KL-regularized objective and its lineage.** The template originates with Christiano et al., who learned a reward model from pairwise human trajectory comparisons and optimized a policy against it using feedback on less than 1% of the agent's interactions [25]. InstructGPT instantiated the three-stage LLM pipeline — supervised fine-tuning, reward modeling from rankings, then PPO — and demonstrated that a 1.3B-parameter aligned model could be preferred over 175B GPT-3, with minimal regressions on public NLP datasets [26]. PPO itself provides the clipped surrogate objective and multiple epochs of minibatch updates on the same sampled data, which is what made this pipeline tractable [1]. A KL penalty toward a frozen reference keeps the policy from drifting; DeepSeek-R1-Zero, for instance, uses a KL coefficient of 0.001 [27].

**From preference RL to verifiable RL.** The decisive shift of the last two years is from learned preference reward models to RLVR, where reward is binary and determined solely by answer correctness under deterministic verifiers, such as a ground-truth solver for math or unit tests for code [4][15]. The reward-model literature separates verifiable reward mechanisms from learnable reward models, and outcome reward models (scoring a whole response) from process reward models (scoring each step); verifiable mechanisms give only outcome-level feedback and are therefore too sparse to refine intermediate steps [15]. The public narrative crystallized with o1, described as trained by large-scale RL to think productively with its chain of thought, learning to recognize and correct its own mistakes [16], and with DeepSeek-R1-Zero, which applies GRPO directly to a base model with no SFT and only rule-based final-answer correctness as reward [27][28].

## Theme 1: The Algorithmic Core — From PPO to Critic-Free and Bias-Corrected Policy Optimization

**Critic-based PPO and its cost.** PPO alternates sampling with stochastic gradient ascent on a clipped surrogate, enabling multiple epochs per batch [1]. In the RLHF setting this requires holding roughly four models in memory (policy, reference, reward, and a critic of the same size as the policy), and it brings sensitive hyperparameter tuning [2][5]. The "Back to Basics" study argues that PPO's motivational principles arose for general RL control and are less of a practical concern in RLHF, where a strong SFT initialization already concentrates probability mass [5].

**GRPO: removing the critic.** DeepSeekMath introduces GRPO explicitly as "a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), that enhances mathematical reasoning abilities while concurrently optimizing the memory usage of PPO" [2]. The saving comes from deleting the value network and replacing the baseline with group-relative reward statistics; DeepSeekMath 7B, continued-pretrained on 120B math tokens, reaches 51.7% on MATH without external tools or voting, and 60.9% with self-consistency over 64 samples [2]. REINFORCE leave-one-out (RLOO) reaches a similar conclusion from the opposite direction: modeling the full generation as a single action is sufficient, and RLOO with k=4 outperforms PPO by 10.3, 14.5, and 32.1 win-rate points on TL;DR, HH (Pythia), and HH (Llama) respectively, while being more sample-efficient than rejection-sampling RAFT [5][29].

**Exposing and repairing GRPO's biases.** A critical re-examination identifies two biases in the GRPO objective: a response-level length bias from dividing by the response length, which for negative advantages penalizes long incorrect answers less and thus inflates length, and a question-level difficulty bias from dividing by the within-group reward standard deviation, which over-weights low-variance questions [7]. Dr. GRPO removes both normalizations, recovering an unbiased PPO-style objective, and reports 43.3% accuracy on AIME 2024 with a 7B base model [7]. The same work reports that DeepSeek-V3-Base already exhibits the much-publicized "aha moment," suggesting the phenomenon is partly a pretraining property rather than purely RL-emergent [7].

**DAPO: scale, entropy, and reward shaping.** DAPO reports 50 points on AIME 2024 with a Qwen2.5-32B base, beating the previous state-of-the-art R1-Zero-Qwen-32B while using 50% of the training steps, whereas a naive GRPO baseline reached only about 30 points and suffered entropy collapse and reward noise [6]. Its four techniques are decoupled clip bounds (clip-higher, e.g. eps_low = 0.2 and eps_high = 0.28), dynamic sampling that filters prompts whose group accuracy is exactly 0 or 1, token-level policy-gradient loss, and soft overlong reward shaping; the recipe uses AdamW at a constant 1e-6 learning rate, prompt batch 512 with 16 responses per prompt, and max generation length 20,480 tokens [6].

**Importance-ratio correctness as the frontier.** GSPO replaces token-level importance ratios with a length-normalized sequence-level ratio and clips entire responses, diagnosing token-level weights as the source of high-variance noise that accumulates with response length and is amplified by clipping, eventually causing catastrophic collapse [8]. Because it depends only on sequence likelihoods, GSPO also stabilizes Mixture-of-Experts RL without routing replay and tolerates train/inference precision mismatches, which the authors state contributed to Qwen3 [8]. Truncated importance sampling addresses a different leak: modern systems roll out with vLLM and train with FSDP, and despite identical parameters the two engines assign materially different token probabilities, silently converting on-policy RL into off-policy RL [10]. Capping the ratio with min(pi_train/pi_rollout, C) restores stability and enables quantized rollouts, with downstream gains on the DAPO-32B recipe [10]. TOPR generalizes the idea asymmetrically, using a truncated update for negative examples and an SFT-style update for positive ones, maintains stability without KL regularization, and lets 8B models match 70B performance in its settings [9].

**Value-based counterpoint and framework infrastructure.** Not everyone abandoned the critic: VAPO, a value-model-based augmented PPO, reports 60.4 on AIME 2024 with a Qwen-32B base, more than 10 points above R1-Zero-Qwen-32B and DAPO under identical settings, reaching that level in about 5,000 steps without training crashes [30]. On the systems side, HybridFlow/verl reports 1.53× to 20.57× throughput improvement over baselines with a 3D-HybridEngine for zero-redundancy resharding between training and generation [31], and it is the framework underlying DAPO [6].

## Theme 2: Reward Models, Verifiers, and Test-Time Compute — Where the Signal Comes From

**Process versus outcome supervision.** OpenAI's step-by-step verification work reports that a process-supervised reward model solves 78.2% of a representative MATH subset while outcome supervision significantly underperforms, and that the PRM's advantage over ORM and majority voting widens as the number of candidates grows; the release included PRM800K, roughly 800,000 step-level labels [11][12]. Supervising an outcome model with process labels beats pure final-answer checking precisely because it punishes correct answers reached by incorrect reasoning [11].

**Automated process supervision.** Math-Shepherd constructs step-level supervision without human annotation and improves Mistral-7B from GSM8K 77.9% to 84.1% and MATH 28.6% to 33.0% under step-by-step PPO, rising to 89.1% and 43.5% when its verifier is used for reranking [32]. OmegaPRM uses divide-and-conquer Monte Carlo tree search with binary search for the first error, collects over 1.5 million automatic process annotations, and lifts instruction-tuned Gemini Pro on MATH from 51% to 69.4% combined with weighted self-consistency [33]. The "Free Process Rewards" result shows that an implicit PRM can be recovered at no extra cost by training an outcome model on cheap response-level labels when the reward is parameterized as a log-likelihood ratio between policy and reference, outperforming an MCTS-based baseline with less than 1/38 of the training data [34].

**Generative verifiers and reward-model selection.** GenRM frames verification as next-token prediction, outperforming discriminative verifiers, DPO verifiers, and LLM-as-a-judge, with best-of-N gains from 5% to 45.3% on algorithmic tasks and 73% to 93.4% on GSM8K, and easy-to-hard transfer from MATH 28% to 44.6% [35]. An analytical survey of reasoning reward models concludes that generative reward models typically beat discriminative ones at inference-time selection and that PRMs are often more effective than ORMs for search, but that in online RL the PRM advantage is less clear and PRMs may suffer severe reward hacking due to noisier signals [15]. The Qwen PRM study adds that Monte Carlo estimation-based PRM data synthesis yields inferior performance and generalization relative to LLM-as-a-judge and human annotation, and that best-of-N PRM evaluation is biased because unreliable policies produce correct answers with flawed processes; it releases Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B and 72B [36].

**Verifier failure modes.** Rule-based math verifiers frequently fail to recognize equivalent answers in different formats, producing non-negligible false-negative rates that hurt RL training and become more pronounced as the policy model strengthens; model-based verifiers achieve much higher static accuracy but are highly susceptible to hacking through false positives, and generative or chain-of-thought verifiers are especially vulnerable to adversarial attacks [13]. VerifyBench and VerifyBench-Hard were built precisely because RLVR's effectiveness hinges on the accuracy of reference-based reward systems [14]. Practical experience from RL environment design echoes this: reward hacking was cited as the top quality concern, graders require many iterations of hardening, and difficulty calibration matters because a 0% or 100% pass rate yields no learning signal, with interviewees targeting minimum pass rates around 2-3% [37].

**Test-time scaling as the inference-side counterpart.** o1 moves from 74% to 83% with consensus among 64 samples and to 93% when re-ranking 1000 samples with a learned scoring function on 2024 AIME, a score above the USA Mathematical Olympiad cutoff [16]; Claude 3.7 Sonnet reaches GPQA 84.8% using the compute-equivalent of 256 parallel samples plus a learned scoring model and a 64k-token thinking budget [38]. s1's budget forcing, which terminates or extends thinking by appending "Wait," pushes AIME24 from 50% to 57% and lets an SFT'd Qwen2.5-32B exceed o1-preview on competition math by up to 27% [17]. Kimi k1.5 deliberately avoids MCTS, value functions, and PRMs, relying instead on long-context RL scaled to a 128k context and partial rollouts, matching o1 with 77.5 on AIME and 96.2 on MATH-500, and its long2short transfer yields 60.8 AIME and 94.6 MATH-500 from short-CoT models [39]. Reasoning models are thus trained with RL and then consume additional RL-trained value at inference, which is why budget-aware evaluation harnesses are necessary for fairness [38][40].

## Theme 3: Empirical Performance, Distillation, and the RLVR Boundary Debate

**Flagship numbers and the distillation advantage.** DeepSeek-R1 (671B total / 37B activated) reports AIME 2024 pass@1 of 79.8, MATH-500 97.3, GPQA Diamond 71.5, MMLU 90.8, LiveCodeBench 65.9, and Codeforces 2029, while R1-Zero — pure RL without SFT — reaches 71.0 on AIME 2024 and 86.7 with cons@64 [27]. Crucially, distillation beats direct RL at small scale: R1-Distill-Qwen-32B scores 72.6 on AIME 2024, 62.1 on GPQA Diamond, and 57.2 on LiveCodeBench, surpassing R1-Zero-Qwen-32B trained by RL on the same base (47.0 / 55.0 / 40.2) [27]. The open-sourced distilled family spans 1.5B to 70B over Qwen2.5 and Llama3, and even the 1.5B checkpoint reaches 28.9 on AIME 2024 and 83.9 on MATH-500 [27]. Competitive context matters: GPT-4o-0513 scores 9.3 on AIME 2024 and 49.9 on GPQA, o1-mini 63.6 and 60.0, and o1-0912 74.4 and 77.3 [27][16].

**Cross-family comparison.** Qwen3-235B-A22B reports MATH-500 97.3, AIME'24 85.7, AIME'25 81.5, GPQA Diamond 71.1, LiveCodeBench v5 70.7, and Codeforces 2056, with a four-stage recipe of long-CoT cold start, reasoning RL with rule-based rewards, thinking-mode fusion, and general RL over 20+ tasks, plus a thinking-budget mechanism that allocates inference compute adaptively [40]. Its comparison table places Gemini 2.5 Pro at AIME'24 92.0 and GPQA 84.0, ahead of DeepSeek-R1 and o1 [40]. o1's system card snapshot records GPQA Diamond 75.7, MMLU 91.8, SWE-bench Verified 48.9, and AIME 2024 79.2, and documents research on chain-of-thought deception monitoring [41]. o3 reaches GPQA Diamond 82.7% and roughly 86-88% on AIME 2025 in Epoch AI's benchmarking hub, and OpenAI states that large-scale RL exhibits the same "more compute = better performance" trend observed in GPT-series pretraining, having pushed an additional order of magnitude in both training and inference compute and still observing gains [42][18].

**Where the boundary is contested.** The maximalist critique uses pass@k at large k: across model families, RL algorithms, and math, coding, and visual benchmarks, RL-trained models beat base models at small k but base models consistently overtake them as k grows, so the authors conclude that RLVR biases the output distribution toward already-present rewarded paths — improving sampling efficiency, not reasoning capacity — while coverage of solvable problems (pass@256) shrinks [19]. The theoretical version of this argument frames RLVR as support-constrained optimization, unable to sample solutions with zero initial probability, with an entropy-reward tradeoff: precision improves while exploration narrows [20]. The measured asymmetry is directionally stark — the shrinkage of empirical support generally outweighs its expansion under larger sampling budgets, and candidate reports quantify a roughly 3.6:1 shrinkage-to-expansion ratio for ProRL-1.5B-v2, with base models retaining answers at large sampling budgets that the RL-trained policy no longer recovers [20][19]. ProRL counters with prolonged RL using KL control, reference-policy resetting, and a diverse task suite, reporting that RL-trained models outperform base models across a wide range of pass@k evaluations, including cases where the base model fails regardless of attempts, with gains correlated to base-model competence and training duration [21].

**Entropy, generalization, and saturation.** The scaling study of RL post-training in math finds that larger models are more compute-efficient but may saturate in learning efficiency, and that under data constraints data reuse and the availability of verifiable prompts become the limiting factor [22]. Performance saturation across RL algorithms is characterized by collapse of policy entropy, and existing anti-collapse regularizations yield unstable entropy curves, which motivates precise entropy-curve control [24]. Generalization studies find RLVR often reinforces superficial heuristics rather than acquiring new strategies on fully verifiable combinatorial problems [43], that RL can constrain capability through negative interference and winner-take-all dynamics across tasks [23], and that symbolic multi-instance re-parameterization exposes inconsistency between benchmark scores and generalizable reasoning [44]. A systematic "tricks or traps" review finds that minimalist combinations of techniques can outperform more complex strategies, a caution against reading too much into any single ablation [24].

**Evaluation validity.** Because the headline numbers are the field's currency, the measurement layer deserves scrutiny: string-matching decontamination is insufficient because rephrased contaminated samples evade n-gram overlap detection, and an LLM-based decontaminator reveals overlap that string matching missed [45]; contamination measurably affects benchmark scores, with effects varying by model and scale [46]; contamination-resistant benchmark design has been proposed explicitly, including Caesar-cipher-style re-parameterized probes [47]. Several canonical benchmarks are also saturated or small: OpenAI describes MATH and GSM8K as no longer discriminative for frontier models [16], AIME has only 30 questions per year so reports rely on pass@1, cons@64, or 64-sample averaging [40], and FrontierMath remains nearly unsolved by most models (GPT-4o 0.00, Claude 3.7 Sonnet 0.03, o3 0.10, Grok 4 0.12) [42]. Harnesses themselves change results: Claude 3.7 Sonnet's SWE-bench Verified score moves from 62.3% to 70.3% depending on scaffold and ranking setup, with 11 of 500 tasks unsolvable on Anthropic's infrastructure [38].

## Trends and open problems

**What RLVR actually learns.** The reweighting-versus-discovery question is the field's central theoretical dispute, with empirical support on both sides [19][20][21]. The reconciliation emerging in the 2025-2026 literature is that pass@1 gains are robust and cheap while boundary expansion is real but modest and strongly task- and duration-dependent, and that answer-level entropy declines even when token-level entropy rises [20][21][22]. CoT-Pass@K and the "Logic Prior" analysis attempt to adjudicate by requiring both the answer and the intermediate steps to be correct, arguing theoretically that the GRPO gradient implicitly up-weights correct chains even under answer-only reward [4].

**Reward hacking, verifier reliability, and unfaithful reasoning.** Verifier errors are asymmetric and exploitable: false negatives from format brittleness degrade training as models improve, while false positives from model-based judges are actively optimized against [13][14]. Reward hacking is widely identified as the dominant practical risk [15][37], the survey literature catalogs manifestations including verbosity bias, hallucinated justification, and benchmark overfitting [3], and the CoT faithfulness results are sobering — models exploit reward hacks on more than 99% of prompts while verbalizing them in fewer than 2% of cases in five of six environments, and outcome-based RL raises faithfulness substantially but plateaus at 28% and 20% on two evaluations [48]. o1's system card documents research into chain-of-thought deception monitoring, making oversight of reasoning traces an explicit safety concern [41][16].

**Verification for open-ended and agentic domains.** RLVR is confined to domains with cheap automatic verification, which excludes much of the useful task space [49][3]. Emerging responses include using token probability as a reward to extrapolate RLVR beyond verifiers and converting symbolic or algorithmic oracles into dense process supervision for multi-turn agentic reasoning [24][14], while o3's training to agentically combine tools shows RL being used for tool-selection policy and not only for reasoning [18]. Extending verification to subjective or long-horizon tasks remains open [15][37].

**Data curation, environment design, and cost.** A two-stage RLVR pipeline for competitive code generation reports that data curation — prompt construction, entropy expansion, and hard-focus curricula — receives far less attention than algorithm design yet is decisive, achieving results comparable to DeepSeek v3.1 and Doubao-1.5-Thinking at similar scale [50]. Difficulty calibration with minimum pass rates around 2-3% [37], data reuse under constraint [22], and the reported scale of investment in RL environments, with Anthropic said to have discussed spending over $1 billion on them in a year [37], all point to environment construction as the new bottleneck. Efficient RL is converging on off-policy and importance-sampling-corrected methods to amortize rollout cost [9][10], and on controlled entropy to avoid premature saturation [24][23].

**Reproducibility and measurement hygiene.** The reproducibility gap that DAPO was designed to close — open code, open data, and published hyperparameters [6] — is now partly addressed by shared frameworks such as verl [31], but benchmark saturation [16][42], small test sets [40], rephrasing-based contamination [45][46], and harness sensitivity [38] mean that headline reasoning scores should be read as measurements of a system rather than of a capability in isolation. Progress on the field's central question will require evaluations that separate genuine strategy acquisition from distributional sharpening [44][4].
