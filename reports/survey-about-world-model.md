# World Models for AI Agents: Foundations, Architectures, Benchmarks and Open Debates

## TL;DR

- A "world model" is a learned predictive model of environment dynamics that supports simulation and decision-making; the canonical formulation decomposes it into a representation model, a latent dynamics model and a small controller, with the explicit design principle that most complexity should sit in the world model rather than the policy [1]. The field has since forked into two research programmes: models that **understand the present** through internal representations (JEPA, Dreamer, MuZero) and models that **predict the future** through generative simulation of pixels [2].
- Five architecture families dominate 2023-2025 work: latent recurrent state-space / imagination agents (PlaNet [3] → DreamerV3 [4], Dreamer 4 [5], TD-MPC2 [6]), autoregressive token-based video world models (Genie [7], iVideoGPT [8], Pandora [9]), diffusion and flow-matching interactive world models (DIAMOND [10], GameNGen [11], GAIA-2 [12]), explicit 3D/geometric world models (Marble [13], BTimer [14]), and non-generative JEPA-style embedding predictors (I-JEPA [15], V-JEPA [16], V-JEPA 2 [17], DINO-WM [18]).
- Architectures that abandon pixel reconstruction are now competitive on control: a single 317M-parameter TD-MPC2 agent is decoder-free and performs 80 tasks across four domains with one hyperparameter set [6], while V-JEPA 2-AC planning costs roughly 16 seconds per action versus roughly 4 minutes per action for an action-conditioned video-generation world model, at higher success on zero-shot Franka pick-and-place (80% vs 15% for Octo) [17].
- Generative fidelity and physical understanding are empirically decoupled. On Physics-IQ the best of the evaluated video models scores only 29.5 out of a physical-variance baseline of 100 [19][20], and WorldModelBench reports mass-conservation and gravity violations that generic video-quality metrics cannot see, with the strongest model completing the instructed task in only 61% of videos and 12% of generations violating mass conservation [21][22].
- Long-horizon rollout remains the field's hardest structural problem: independent diagnostics report imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error roughly 180x above matched real-physics rollouts for DreamerV3 on walker-walk [23], and theory shows that imperfect world models are *exploitable* in model-based planning, effectively unavoidably so on large policy sets [24]. The dominant workarounds (RL on self-generated rollouts, drift-aware aggregation) are mitigation, not solution [25].

## Background

**The canonical definition.** The modern deep-learning notion of a world model was popularized by Ha and Schmidhuber, who train a generative neural network unsupervised to learn a compressed spatial and temporal representation of an environment [1]. Their architecture separates a vision model (V, a convolutional VAE compressing each frame into a latent `z_t`), a memory model (M, an MDN-RNN that models the distribution `P(z_{t+1} | a_t, z_t, h_t)` as a mixture of diagonal Gaussians), and a tiny controller (C, in its simplest form a single linear layer `a_t = W_c [z_t h_t] + b_c`) trained separately, for example with CMA-ES [1]. Two design commitments from that paper persist: dynamics is modelled as a *distribution* over futures rather than a point prediction, so the controller can be trained inside a stochastic "dream"; and most model capacity is deliberately allocated to V and M rather than to the policy [1]. The idea has older roots in Schmidhuber's model-building control systems, where a controller is equipped with a world-model module predicting future inputs from prior input-action pairs [2].

**Latent state-space dynamics and value equivalence.** PlaNet made the model-based loop purely latent: it learns an encoder plus a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) with *both* a deterministic recurrent path and a stochastic path, and plans online by model-predictive control in that latent space from pixels only [3]. The ablation is instructive: the deterministic component carries information across many steps and the stochastic component is indispensable -- "the agent does not learn without" it, so neither a purely deterministic GRU nor a purely stochastic SSM suffices [3]. PlaNet introduces *latent overshooting*, a multi-step variational objective that enforces consistency between one-step and multi-step latent predictions [3]. MuZero took the orthogonal route of **value equivalence**: it learns an iterable latent dynamics model whose prediction function outputs only reward, policy and value, never reconstructing observations, and matches AlphaZero on Go, chess and shogi without being given the rules while setting a new state of the art on 57 Atari games [26]. EfficientZero refined this recipe with a self-supervised temporal-consistency objective, a learned "value prefix", and model-based correction of off-policy value targets, reaching 194.3% mean / 109.0% median human-normalized score on Atari 100k -- roughly two hours of real-time experience, versus the 200M-frame regime of DQN [27]. This lineage establishes the first major taxonomic axis: **reconstruction-based** latent dynamics versus **task-aligned / value-equivalent** latent dynamics.

**The non-generative branch.** LeCun's Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture defines the opposite commitment: rather than generating the target signal `y`, a JEPA learns to predict the *embedding* of `y` from the embedding of a context signal `x` using a predictor conditioned on a latent variable `z`, with no decoder required [15]. I-JEPA instantiates this for images by predicting the representations of several target blocks from a single context block under an L2 loss in representation space, using an asymmetric EMA target encoder to prevent representation collapse -- explicitly *not* reconstructing pixels the way a masked autoencoder does [15]. V-JEPA extends the paradigm to video with a pure feature-prediction objective and no pretrained image encoder, text, negatives or pixel reconstruction, reporting 81.9% on Kinetics-400, 72.2% on Something-Something-v2 and 77.9% on ImageNet-1K with a ViT-H/16 trained on roughly 2M videos [16] and arguing that discarding unpredictable information yields training and sample-efficiency gains of roughly 1.5x to 6x over generative counterparts [28]. V-JEPA 2 (1.2B parameters, pretrained on more than 1M hours of internet video) then post-trains an action-conditioned latent world model, V-JEPA 2-AC, on fewer than 62 hours of unlabeled Droid robot video, and achieves zero-shot pick-and-place on Franka arms at 65%-80% success, described by its authors as the first video-trained world model enabling state-of-the-art understanding, prediction and zero-shot robot planning [17][29]. V-JEPA is also mirrored on the Hugging Face papers index [30], and I-JEPA and DIAMOND likewise appear in the Hugging Face paper index [31], reflecting how the community tracks this literature.

**Why the definition is contested.** Survey taxonomies converge on a dichotomy rather than a single definition: world models are treated "as tools for either understanding the present state of the world or predicting its future dynamics" [2]. Ding et al. split the literature into implicit/internal representation construction (the JEPA line and the Dreamer/MuZero decision-making tradition) and generative future-state prediction (Sora-style video generation), explicitly noting that the definition "remains a subject of ongoing debate" [2]. Other surveys add their own axes: a four-axis scheme spanning architecture, methodological family, reasoning strategy and application domain [2]; a three-axis scheme for embodied agents based on functionality (decision-coupled vs general-purpose), temporal modelling (sequential simulation vs global difference prediction) and spatial representation (global latent vector, token feature sequence, spatial latent grid, decomposed rendering) [32]; and a three-tier taxonomy for autonomous driving [33]; a Sora-focused survey instead organizes the field by application into video generation, autonomous driving and autonomous agents, and is widely indexed as a Hugging Face paper [34]. World Labs offers a functional cut instead -- **renderers** (output pixels), **simulators** (output state, requiring geometric and physical faithfulness) and **planners** (output actions) -- arguing the three are already blending [35].

**Why it matters.** The practical motivation is sample efficiency and planning in imagination: model-free agents need millions of environment interactions while latent world models reach comparable performance in a fraction of the data [3][27], and imagination-based training lets an agent improve without further environment interaction at all [4][5]. DeepMind frames world models as "a key stepping stone on the path to AGI" because they enable training in unlimited simulated curricula [23]; NVIDIA frames them as the digital twin of the world required by "physical AI", alongside the policy model as a digital twin of the agent [36].

## Theme 1: Latent Imagination Agents and Value-Equivalent Models

The most mature family learns a compact latent dynamics model and trains behaviour inside it. **DreamerV3** learns an RSSM world model (an encoder producing a stochastic categorical `z_t` with straight-through gradients, and a recurrent sequence model producing `h_t`) and improves behaviour by imagining future scenarios; with a *single fixed configuration* it masters more than 150 diverse tasks and is the first method to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [4]. Its stabilizers are worth quoting because they recur throughout the family: a categorical latent vector rather than a Gaussian, and "free bits" that clip the dynamics and representation losses below roughly 1 nat (about 1.44 bits) to prevent a degenerate, predictable-but-uninformative latent; the loss weights are fixed across domains at `beta_pred = 1`, `beta_dyn = 1`, `beta_rep = 0.1` [4]. On Atari 100k (26 games, 400K frames, roughly two hours of play) DreamerV3 reports a human-normalized mean of 112% and median of 49% [37]; on the 200M-frame Atari suite its median is 302% versus DreamerV2's 219%, and its DMLab data-efficiency gain over IMPALA is over 13,000% (50M steps versus 10B) [4].

**Dreamer 4** moves the family from online RL to fully offline RL *inside* the model: the policy is trained entirely in imagination without environment interaction, it is the first agent to obtain Minecraft diamonds purely from offline data, and it must therefore handle sequences of more than 20,000 low-level mouse and keyboard actions [5]. The enabling techniques are a shortcut-forcing objective and an efficient transformer that together allow real-time interactive world-model inference on a single GPU [5].

**TD-MPC2** attacks the same problem with a *decoder-free* implicit world model and local trajectory optimization in latent space [6]. Because nothing reconstructs pixels, capacity is spent on the quantities control needs; a single 317M-parameter agent performs 80 tasks spanning DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2 and MyoSuite -- differing embodiments and action spaces, up to `A` in `R^39` -- with one hyperparameter set, trained on 545M transitions, and capabilities increase with model and data size rather than saturating, with normalized score scaling roughly linearly in the log of parameters [6]. This is a concrete scaling result: one agent, one configuration, 104 continuous-control tasks in the broader suite, and about a 300x parameter increase over TD-MPC [6].

**Transformer and contrastive variants** close the gap with model-free methods. TWISTER combines a transformer world model with action-conditioned contrastive predictive coding and reports 162% mean / 77% median human-normalized score on Atari 100k, the strongest model-based result without look-ahead search, versus DreamerV3's 112%/49% and DIAMOND's 146% [37]. DIAMOND trains the RL agent inside a *diffusion* world model and reaches 1.46 human-normalized mean on Atari 100k, a new best at the time for agents trained entirely inside a world model, while simultaneously acting as a playable neural game engine [10]. iVideoGPT unifies observations, actions and rewards into a single token sequence and pretrains on millions of human and robotic manipulation trajectories, supporting action-conditioned video prediction, visual planning and model-based RL [8].

**Interpreting the family.** The empirical pattern is that latent, non-reconstructing world models trade visual fidelity for control utility and win on cost. V-JEPA 2-AC needs roughly 16 seconds per action with 800 samples, whereas an action-conditioned video-generating world model based on Cosmos needs roughly 4 minutes per action with 80 samples for *lower* success [17]. Against this, the diagnostic literature finds the family's imagination is not physically faithful: an independent study reports imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error approximately 180x above matched real-physics rollouts at horizon 16 for DreamerV3 on walker-walk, with the imagined-rollout error statistically *flat* across a friction sweep that collapses the real trained policy's reward -- evidence that the model extrapolates kinematics without capturing contact dynamics [23].

## Theme 2: Generative World Models over Pixels, Tokens and Latents

The second family predicts observations, not just task-relevant latents, and is where the largest compute budgets and the most visible public claims live.

**Autoregressive token models.** Genie is an 11B-parameter foundation world model trained unsupervised on 30,000 hours of unlabeled 2D-platformer gameplay; its components are a spatiotemporal video tokenizer (ST-ViViT), an autoregressive MaskGIT dynamics model, and an unsupervised latent action model whose VQ codebook contains only 8 latent actions -- enough to let a user drive a generated environment [7]. Genie's scaling study spans 40M to 2.7B parameters with a 200M-parameter tokenizer and 300M-parameter latent action model, 16-frame sequences at 10 FPS, and 25 MaskGIT sampling steps per frame [7]. Pandora is a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion model that couples a pretrained 7B LLM (Vicuna-7B-v1.5) with a pretrained video model to enable on-the-fly *free-text* action control at any timestep, trained on roughly 500K re-captioned video-text pairs plus 260K action-annotated examples, and generating 8-second (64-frame) videos from clips of at most 5 seconds [9].

**Diffusion and flow-matching engines.** GameNGen is the first game engine powered entirely by a neural model: a diffusion model predicts the next DOOM frame at 20 FPS on a single TPU, remains stable over multi-minute sessions, achieves next-frame PSNR 29.4 (comparable to lossy JPEG), and leaves human raters only slightly better than chance at distinguishing real from simulated clips even after five minutes of autoregressive generation; training proceeds in two phases, first recording an RL agent's play and then learning next-frame prediction [11]. Oasis (Decart and Etched) applies a ViT-based spatial autoencoder plus a DiT-based latent diffusion backbone, generates 20 FPS output (one frame every 0.04 seconds, more than 100x faster than Sora-class models), and releases a 500M-parameter open-weights checkpoint; its authors note pointedly that "there is no physics engine; just a foundation model" [24]. GAIA-2 is an 8.4B-parameter space-time factorized transformer using flow matching to generate up to five synchronized camera streams at 448x960, with 22 transformer blocks, hidden size 4096 and 32 heads, trained for 460,000 steps at batch size 256 on 256 H100 GPUs over 48-frame inputs at 20/25/30 Hz [12][38]; GAIA-1 established the generative driving world model recipe conditioned on video, text and action [39]. Vista is a generalizable driving world model supporting both high-level controls (command, goal point) and low-level manoeuvres (trajectory, angle, speed), outperforming the best prior driving world model by 55% FID and 27% FVD [40].

**Explicit 3D and real-time renderers.** Genie 3 generates dynamic, navigable worlds in real time at 720p and 24 FPS with consistency over a few minutes, and its environmental consistency is **emergent** from frame-by-frame autoregressive generation, explicitly not relying on an explicit 3D representation as NeRFs and Gaussian Splatting do [23]. World Labs takes the opposite bet with RTFM, a real-time generative world model that renders 2D images of a scene from new viewpoints without building any explicit 3D representation, implemented as an autoregressive diffusion transformer targeting a single H100 GPU, with worlds that persist regardless of interaction length [25]. Its Marble product instead produces persistent, navigable, exportable 3D worlds as Gaussian splats, meshes or videos, advertised as having "no time limits, no morphing, no inconsistency" [13]. On the reconstruction side, feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting can lift a monocular video into an explicit dynamic 3D representation in roughly 150 ms [14]. The explicit-versus-emergent dichotomy is thus a live architectural disagreement rather than a settled matter [23][25][13].

**JEPA-style non-generative world models at scale.** V-JEPA 2 reaches 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2, 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100, 84.0 on PerceptionTest and 76.9 on TempCompass [17]. DINO-WM builds its world model on frozen DINOv2 patch features, predicts future patch features from offline trajectories, and achieves zero-shot goal-reaching by action-sequence optimization across six environments without expert demonstrations or a learned inverse model [18]. The two variants differ in what is held fixed -- DINO-WM inherits a frozen visual backbone, whereas V-JEPA 2 trains its encoder -- but both demonstrate that test-time planning in embedding space is viable without any pixel decoder [17][18].

## Theme 3: Benchmarks, Head-to-Head Numbers and Failure Modes

**A layered benchmark stack.** Evaluation has stratified into four layers. *Sample-efficient RL*: Atari 100k (26 games, 400K frames, human-normalized return) and the DeepMind Control Suite, whose 39 tasks TD-MPC2 uses [6][37]. *Multi-task and transfer*: TD-MPC2's 104-task continuous-control suite [6] and LIBERO, the standard lifelong manipulation benchmark with 130 tasks across LIBERO-Spatial, -Object, -Goal and LIBERO-100 (itself split into LIBERO-90 for pretraining plus LIBERO-Long for downstream evaluation) [41]. *Driving*: NAVSIM, which uses a non-reactive data-driven simulator and a PDMS score in the unit interval aggregated from no-collision, drivable-area compliance, time-to-collision, comfort and ego-progress over a 4-second horizon [42]. *Generative world models*: WorldScore, the "first unified benchmark for world generation", with 3,000 test examples and metrics for controllability, quality and dynamics [43][44]; WorldModelBench, which evaluates video generators as world models across 7 domains, 56 subdomains and 350 prompts using 67K human labels and a fine-tuned 2B judger that beats GPT-4o by 8.6% average accuracy at predicting world-modelling violations [21][22]; Physics-IQ, with 396 videos across solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, optics, thermodynamics and magnetism normalized so that real-video physical variance equals 100 [19][20]; VBench, which decomposes video-generation quality into 16 disentangled dimensions [45][46]; and Morpheus, which benchmarks physical reasoning against real physical experiments [47].

**Concrete head-to-head results.** On Atari 100k, DreamerV3 reports 112% mean / 49% median [37], DIAMOND 146% [10] and TWISTER 162%/77% [37]; on Atari 200M DreamerV3's median is 302% against DreamerV2's 219% [4]. GameNGen holds 20 FPS with PSNR 29.4 on DOOM [11]. V-JEPA 2-AC beats Octo on zero-shot Franka pick-and-place, 80% versus 15% for cup pick-and-place and 65% versus 10% for box [17]. On NAVSIM's navtest split, TransFuser and PARA-Drive both reach 84.0 PDMS, UniAD 83.4, a camera-only LTF planner 83.8, an ego-status-only MLP 65.6, and a constant-velocity baseline about 20.6, against roughly 95 for a human operator -- a gap of about ten points between the best learned planners and humans, with the CVPR-2024 challenge winner Hydra-MDP reaching about 91-92.7 [42]. On Physics-IQ, the best model (VideoPoet-multiframe) scores 29.5, ahead of Lumiere-multiframe 23.0, Runway Gen 3 22.8 and Sora image-to-video 10.0 [19]. On WorldScore's leaderboard, 3D scene generators lead the static ranking -- Voyager at 77.62, WonderWorld 72.69, LucidDreamer 70.40 -- largely because they natively support camera control, while the best open-source video model reaches only about 40 on camera control (CogVideoX-T2V, with CogVideoX-I2V at 62.15 overall) [43][44].

**Failure modes.** Five recur across the literature.

1. *Autoregressive drift and exposure bias.* Teacher forcing trains on clean history while inference conditions on the model's own outputs, so errors compound within seconds and object identity dissolves or end-effectors drift from commanded trajectories. Mitigations include RL post-training on self-generated rollouts (reported LPIPS improvement of 14% on external cameras and SSIM improvement of 9.1% on a wrist camera on DROID, with a 98% win rate in paired comparisons), DAgger-style aggregation of backward-perturbed histories, and reverse-prediction cycle consistency [25]. Genie 3's own documentation acknowledges that autoregressive inaccuracies accumulate over time [23].

2. *Visual realism is not physical understanding.* Physics-IQ finds physical understanding "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism", with the best model at 29.5 out of 100 and Sora frequently inserting transition cuts despite static-camera instructions [19][20]; WorldModelBench likewise flags mass-conservation and gravity violations invisible to generic video-quality benchmarks [21][22]; Morpheus finds that current models struggle to encode physical principles despite aesthetically pleasing output, and criticizes trajectory-matching metrics for false negatives and VLM judging for hallucination risk [47].

3. *"Kinematic, not dynamic" imagination and OOD collapse.* Beyond the 180x kinematic-consistency error result [23], an ICML 2025 study finds video models generalize within distribution and show measurable scaling for combinatorial generalization but fail out of distribution, behaving "case-based" by mimicking the nearest training example -- concluding that "scaling alone is insufficient for video generation models to uncover fundamental physical laws" [48].

4. *Evaluation gaps and proxy assumptions.* WorldScore motivates itself by noting that existing benchmarks score only single-scene quality and cannot distinguish multi-scene world generation [43]; WorldModelBench notes that prior benchmarks "focus only on general video quality, ignoring physics adherence" [21]; NAVSIM's own paper cautions that non-reactive simulation is a strong assumption and PDMS only a proxy, with the ego-status-only agent at 65.6 PDMS showing how much signal leaks from kinematic state alone [42]; and Physics-IQ has itself been audited by a follow-up that reports shortcomings and proposes fixes [19].

5. *Model exploitation and reward hacking.* Theory shows imperfect world models are exploitable because the proxy transition model can *invert* the true policy ordering; exploitation is "essentially unavoidable on large policy sets", unlike standard reward hacking it persists even on finite policy sets, and only a relaxed epsilon-exploitability notion with a "safe horizon" can be guaranteed [24]. Reward-model hacking -- an agent planning to reach out-of-distribution high-reward states -- is argued to be a potentially catastrophic and fundamental problem for reward learning [25].

## Trends and open problems

**Industry convergence on "world foundation models" and physical AI.** NVIDIA's Cosmos platform (announced 6 January 2025, with the technical report appearing 7 January 2025) packages generative world foundation models, tokenizers, guardrails and a video processing pipeline; it frames physical AI as needing both a digital twin of the agent (the policy model) and a digital twin of the world (the world model), spans 4B-14B parameter models trained on 9,000 trillion tokens drawn from 20 million hours of video, and includes Cosmos-Predict1, Cosmos-Transfer1 for controllable multimodal world generation and Cosmos-Reason1 for physically grounded multimodal reasoning [49][36]. DeepMind's Genie 3 is positioned as the frontier of real-time interactivity, defining world models as systems whose understanding lets agents "predict both how an environment will evolve and how their actions will affect it" and treating them as a stepping stone to AGI [23]. World Labs pursues spatial intelligence through implicit real-time rendering (RTFM) and explicit simulator-style products (Marble) [25][13][35], Decart and Etched ship open weights for real-time interactive video [24], and Wayve's GAIA-2 generates rare safety-critical driving edge cases -- motivating statistics such as a crash occurring approximately once every 535,000 miles driven [12][38].

**Debate 1: are video generators world models?** Physics-IQ frames the split between proponents, who argue next-frame prediction forces physical understanding by analogy with next-token prediction in LLMs, and sceptics, who note that passive observation cannot reveal the causal effects of interventions and that training data is the digital rather than the embodied world [19]. Gary Marcus's widely read 2024 critique argued that Sora "has not learned physics", fails at object permanence, and that its glitches resemble lossy-compression artefacts and hallucination rather than a data deficit [50], while OpenAI's own report frames scaling video generation as "a promising path towards building general purpose simulators of the physical world" while conceding inaccurate physics such as glass shattering [51]. The Sora survey reads Sora as showing incipient comprehension of physical laws [52].

**Debate 2: reconstruction versus non-generative prediction.** LeCun's position paper proposes JEPA and Hierarchical JEPA as "non-generative architecture[s] for predictive world models" that predict `s_y` from `s_x` while "eschewing the need to predict every detail of y" [15][16]. The counter-position is articulated in a 2025 *Critique of World Model*, which argues that a world model "is not fundamentally about generating videos, but about serving as a sandbox for reasoning and thought-experiment", while also rejecting the opposite extreme -- that world models must *operate* in pixel space -- on the grounds that framing the choice as next-representation versus next-observation prediction creates a false dichotomy, since generative reconstruction objectives tether learned representations to the observable world. The same critique observes that JEPA-family evidence has "mainly been demonstrated in toy environments" while conceding that V-JEPA 2 is a step forward for robotic manipulation [53].

**Debate 3: explicit 3D versus implicit 2D latents.** Genie 3's consistency is emergent without an explicit 3D representation [23] and RTFM deliberately avoids one [25], whereas Marble exports Gaussian splats and collision meshes and the World Labs taxonomy argues renderers alone "fall apart" when queried structurally -- "the model carries no explicit understanding of three-dimensional structure. It produces what a viewer would see, not what is" [13][35].

**Debate 4: is interactivity the right marker?** The *Critique of World Model* argues video-generation models "only generate fixed trajectories and do not support interactions based on alternative actions", lack explicit notions of state and action, and provide no simulation control for counterfactual reasoning, and therefore "fall outside the definition of world models for reasoning and planning" [53]; Genie 3 and Oasis are positioned by their makers precisely on real-time interactivity as the differentiator [23][24].

**Debate 5: do scaling laws exist for world models?** The Sora survey claims emergent abilities in video generation suggest scaling laws in the visual field [52], and both NVIDIA and DeepMind emphasize scalable training [23][36]. Counter-evidence comes from the ICML 2025 physical-law study: in-distribution generalization is near-perfect and combinatorial generalization scales, but OOD performance fails and "scaling alone is insufficient ... to uncover fundamental physical laws" [48].

**Open problems.** Long-horizon consistency and drift remains the core modelling difficulty: Genie 3 supports "a few minutes of continuous interaction, rather than extended hours" with visual memory reaching about one minute back [23], and the embodied-AI survey names long-horizon temporal consistency under error accumulation as an open challenge [32]. Physical and causal grounding is unresolved, with physical understanding decoupled from visual realism and top scores near 30/100 [19][47]. Evaluation needs metrics for physical consistency over pixel fidelity, and suffers from a scarcity of unified datasets [32]; multi-turn interaction benchmarks remain new and no model is strong across all dimensions [32][21]. Memory and long context, real-time interactivity at scale, and the trade-off between performance and the computational efficiency required for real-time control are all open [32][23][24][25]. Generalization to unseen dynamics and to environments with long-term dependencies is questioned for both video and JEPA-family models [53][48]. Safety concerns include hallucinated dynamics and hallucinated objects [19], physics violations that resemble hallucination [50], mitigation through guardrails and watermarking [36], and formally unavoidable model exploitation that bounds safe planning horizons [24][25]. Integration with LLM and VLM reasoning is an active direction: Cosmos-Reason1 supplies physically grounded responses with ontologies for physical common sense and embodied reasoning [36][49], LeCun's architecture couples the world model with a configurable cost module and a hierarchical planner [15], and survey authors argue a unified multimodal world model remains elusive, proposing integration of 3D generation, embodied intelligence and external rule systems [52][2]. DeepMind additionally notes that accurately modelling interactions between multiple independent agents in a shared environment is still an ongoing challenge [23]. Finally, World Labs identifies data imbalance as the field's defining problem: renderers are "awash in internet video while simulators and planners face acute shortages of 3D assets and robot demonstrations", and "optimizing for visual beauty can sacrifice the precision a robot or a high-fidelity simulation needs" [35].

**Where the field is converging.** The three functional roles are blending: V-JEPA 2-AC is a planner built on a predictive model [17], TD-MPC2 is a decoder-free simulator coupled to trajectory optimization [6], DIAMOND is a renderer used as an RL training substrate [10], and GAIA-2 is a renderer serving as a simulator for policy evaluation [12]. Whether a single unified world model can serve as renderer, simulator and planner simultaneously -- with the physical and causal fidelity that simulators and planners demand, and without the data asymmetry that currently favours renderers -- is the central question the 2025 literature poses and does not answer [23][13][35].
